The Corridors of Hadrian

A novel — Eko Svenningsson

v25028 chapters~82,000 wordsEnglishIn editorial review
After closing, the Museum kept a second set of hours. The doors closed to the public at six, and after that the building ran itself: security lights, climate control, relays clicking softly in the walls.

Emma Voss is a conservator who would rather document a gap than paper over it. Working late on a bequest the Museum is rushing toward display, she follows a sound into the Ancient Near East galleries and finds six strangers chanting over a cylinder seal she catalogued days before, while a seventh man watches from outside the circle. When she reaches into the light above the seal, it rises out of her to meet it. The man knows her name. The card she slaps away is back in her pocket before she reaches the elevator.

In Crown Heights, Miles Ashford, an engineer who once taught the structures beneath Hadrian’s buildings, keeps his life standing one tower of books at a time. When the last boxes of their dead mentor, Thomas Keane, block his only aisle, he opens one. Inside: photographs that place the same man at the edge of excavations from 1979 to 2024, a line in block capitals, not the donor — the handler, and, beneath an acquisition code, the name he never lets himself say.

The Corridors of Hadrian is a literary conspiracy thriller about what institutions can hide in plain sight, and about two careful people who must decide what they believe about each other before they can decide what they believe about the light. Its footnotes form the characters’ field file, keeping the scholarship they consult distinct from the mechanism invented for the novel.

The manuscript is in editorial review. The sneak preview is the opening as it now stands; the full manuscript is available to agents and publishers by request.
