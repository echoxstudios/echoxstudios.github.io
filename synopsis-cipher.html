The Corridors of Hadrian

A novel — Eko Svenningsson

v259 ~83,000 words 29 chapters English In editorial review
Professional overview

On the last evening of October 2025, Museum conservator Emma Voss stays late with a bequest she argued should not yet be displayed: nine cylinder seals from the Castellanos estate, already waiting in a new-acquisitions vitrine for a donor reception. She follows a sound into the Ancient Near East wing and finds six people chanting around the opened case, while a seventh watches from outside the circle. When she reaches into the light above the seal, it rises out of her to meet it. The man outside the circle knows her name, and asks how long she has been in love with someone impossible. His card, which she refuses, is in her pocket when she reaches the freight elevator.

On a quiet brownstone block in Crown Heights, Miles Ashford, an engineer and former evening lecturer on ancient infrastructure, holds himself together by keeping his towers of books in balance; to him they are not clutter but the last load-bearing walls he has. Small silver-scaled watchers have visited his shelves for years. The papers of Thomas Keane, the mentor he and Emma shared, arrive from the estate: photographs that place the same man, Markus Thorne, at the edge of excavations from 1979 to 2024; four words repeated through the notebooks—Corridor. Witness. Recurrence. Solstice.—and, beneath an acquisition code, Emma’s name. His phone log says he called her on the night of the gallery. He does not remember deciding to.

Four months after the memorial where they last spoke for ninety seconds, they meet in a café, both, as it happens, in turtlenecks. She shows him a card that will not crease. He shows her a coin that appeared in his pocket after a street show in Washington Square. By nightfall they are standing outside a locked chapel in the Bronx, beside the buried cornerstone of a home for children, and by the time Emma is home, the photographs she took there show a time she did not take them.

The Corridors of Hadrian is an adult literary-conspiracy thriller with a supernatural premise. Its footnotes form the protagonists’ field file, preserving the difference between the scholarship they consult and the mechanism invented for the novel. It is a book about infrastructure that survives by being called something else.

The manuscript is in editorial review. This overview covers the opening only; the complete synopsis is supplied to agents and publishers with the manuscript.
