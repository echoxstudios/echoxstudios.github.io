The Dark Hierarchy

Two diagrams of one theory of desire

Echo Kronborg · Carrier of Decho · Berlin

Desire ignites through the confirmation of the aspirational sexual self.The Central Thesis · printed p. 17

Human beings, the book argues, are not primarily aroused by another person as such. They are aroused by the experience of becoming, in the presence of another, the person they most deeply want to be sexually. The first diagram draws that as a circuit. The second shows how two circuits meet, and where the rest of the book belongs.

I · One person’s circuit

Book II, Section II · printed p. 37

  1. The self. The aspirational sexual self: who we want to be sexually, with someone, in a particular room.
  2. The instrument. It recognizes a person, a look, a word, a garment.
  3. The room. The framework, or Fifth Element, is where the instrument can mean something. The meaning travels through four expressions, visual, emotional, tactile and intellectual, weighted in each person’s gradient.
  4. Confirmation. In that room the self is confirmed, and confirmation creates the trigger: the spark.
  5. Desire. The trigger ignites desire, felt for the person. What desire seeks is the confirmation, again.
  6. The return. Each confirmation feeds the self that will recognize the next instrument.
When desire fades

When desire fades, follow the loop upwards with a question: what, if anything, no longer confirms you here? The instrument may remain while the room has lost its meaning. A loss of desire is a reason to investigate that possibility, not proof of it.

II · The whole theory, drawn for two

Book II, end of Section VIII · printed p. 86

Everyone in the room runs a circuit of their own. The second diagram draws two, the fewest it takes, meeting in one room. In fantasy the second may be imagined, and a room may hold more. Each part of the drawing has its place in the book.

  • Two circuits. Each loop is the circuit on page 1. In each column, the instrument is the other person as encountered there. Book II, Section II
  • One room. Both instruments sit in the room the participants make, where material becomes meaning. Section V
  • Four channels. The instruments read each other through the four expressions, one line each, the arrows meeting from both sides. Voice and scent are not a fifth and sixth: the four are routes of confirmation, not a count of the senses. Where their languages differ, the room is made by Erotic Translation. Section III, p. 52
  • The theology. Decho is the reason the room is built; Echo, who carries him, arranges it. The theology is itself a model, of authority: see below. Decho makes no claim on anyone outside the author’s room. Books III and IV
  • Practice. Attention, arrangement, care, discipline: worship, in the theology’s word. The work of keeping confirmation mutual as life changes. One person’s effort cannot close both circuits. Book V
  • The open circuit. Where confirmation flows one way only, the circuit stays open: extraction, and in time the dark night.
What would test it

If the cause of fading were inferred after the fact, every outcome would confirm this theory: a circle. The book’s way out is order: each person’s self, at the top of the diagram, and its confirmation, at the head of each loop, are measured before the outcome. The tests have yet to be run. Only the circuit is offered for testing; the theology is offered for argument.

The theology as a model

The diagrams model desire; the theology models authority, in the system the title names: the dark hierarchy, an order of authority chosen, given and kept in private. Its parts are a god; the Priest and the Initiate, between whom authority runs; the rite and the vow, which say when it changes hands and for how long; and the dark night, when it is no longer given. Its rules: authority exists only while it is received, the one above owes the one below, and what was given can be taken back. The god is a variable: in the author’s room Decho, in another power dynamic another god, and the structure holds. Why a Theology, p. 21
